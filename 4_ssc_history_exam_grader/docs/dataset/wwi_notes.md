# World War I practice notes

These short notes, questions, rubrics and reference marks are AI-prepared
teaching materials, pending human review. They are not official SSC questions
or an official marking scheme. This project uses short written answers to
learn how to evaluate an LLM grader.

## Causes and escalation

Nationalism, imperial competition, militarism and alliances help organize the
long-term causes. They did not make war automatic. Franz Ferdinand's murder
at Sarajevo on 28 June 1914 triggered a crisis. Austria-Hungary declared war on
Serbia on 28 July. Russian mobilization, German declarations and Germany's
invasion of Belgium widened the war. Britain entered on 4 August.

Review: [Alabama education lesson](https://accessdl.state.al.us/AventaCourses/access_courses/ushistory11_ua_v17/04_unit/04-01/04-01_learn_text.htm),
[NAM outbreak](https://www.nam.ac.uk/explore/to-arms-1914),
[NAM timeline](https://ww1.nam.ac.uk/timeline/).

## Opposing camps

Britain, France and Russia were the main Entente powers in 1914; Germany and
Austria-Hungary were the core Central Powers. Italy initially stayed neutral,
then joined the Allied side in 1915. Ottoman participation on the Central side
began later in 1914; Bulgaria joined in 1915. The US entered alongside the
Allies in 1917. The pre-war Triple Alliance was not identical to the wartime
Central camp.

Review: [NAM overview](https://www.nam.ac.uk/explore/first-world-war),
[NAM timeline](https://ww1.nam.ac.uk/timeline/),
[US Office of the Historian](https://history.state.gov/milestones/1914-1920/paris-peace).

## Trenches and stalemate

Defensive trenches became extensive on the Western Front by late 1914.
Machine guns, artillery and wire made exposed attacks costly. Repeated
assaults could achieve little ground. Trenches provided protection, but mud,
water, vermin and illness made life difficult. Stalemate was prolonged, not
absolute: weapons, tactics and battlefield conditions changed.

Review: [NAM weapons](https://www.nam.ac.uk/explore/weapons-western-front),
[NAM diary account](https://www.nam.ac.uk/manchester-sergeants-diary-reveals-bitter-struggle-somme-warfare).

## US entry and the armistice

The US declared war on Germany on 6 April 1917. Renewed unrestricted
submarine warfare threatened American shipping, while the Zimmermann
Telegram proposed German-Mexican cooperation against the US. American
troops and resources strengthened an existing Allied effort. The armistice
with Germany on 11 November 1918 stopped fighting; it was not the final
peace settlement, and related conflicts continued elsewhere.

Review: [US entry](https://history.state.gov/milestones/1914-1920/wwi),
[National Archives](https://www.archives.gov/topics/wwi),
[UK government armistice account](https://history.blog.gov.uk/2018/11/09/the-war-that-did-not-end-at-11am-on-11-november/).

## Versailles

Germany signed with the Allied and Associated Powers on 28 June 1919.
Terms included territorial and colonial losses, military restrictions,
reparations obligations and the League Covenant. Restrictions included a
100,000-man army and Rhineland demilitarization. Article 231 supplied a
responsibility basis for reparations; the total payment figure came later.
Resentment mattered, but another war also depended on later economic and
political developments. It was not inevitable from this treaty alone.

## How to use the marking scheme

Each question is worth 10 marks: five content dimensions worth 0, 1 or 2.
Award marks for what the answer demonstrates, not the student's fictional
personality or its length. Equivalent accurate wording is acceptable.
Omission differs from contradiction; a confident factual error is not full
credit. Do not require facts outside the supplied dimension anchors.

The separate reference-grade file contains simulated reference judgements.
Human review should check those judgements against these sources and rubrics
before the project is used to assess agreement with a real human grader.
